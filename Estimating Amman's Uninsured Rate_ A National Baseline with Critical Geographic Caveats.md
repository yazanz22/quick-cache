# Estimating Amman's Uninsured Rate: A National Baseline with Critical Geographic Caveats

## National-Level Health Insurance Coverage in Jordan

A comprehensive assessment of the uninsured population in Amman necessitates first establishing a baseline from national-level data, as no direct statistical measure for the capital city is available in the provided sources. The most detailed and methodologically robust estimate for the Kingdom of Jordan indicates that approximately 76.80% of the total Jordanian population is covered by some form of healthcare insurance system [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. This figure, derived from an analysis of official sources, represents the most reliable foundation for estimating health insurance penetration across the country. From this coverage rate, a corresponding uninsured rate for Jordanians can be calculated: $100\% - 76.80\% = 23.20\%$. This means that nearly one-quarter of the Jordanian population lacks formal health insurance coverage at the national level, representing a significant gap in access to care.

The structure of this 76.80% coverage rate reveals a multi-layered healthcare financing system. The largest component, accounting for 64.3% of the total Jordanian population, is provided by government-run public and semi-governmental insurance systems [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. These include the Ministry of Health (MOH), the Royal Medical Services (RMS) which serves military personnel and their families, and university hospitals [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. This extensive public network forms the backbone of healthcare provision for the majority of citizens. A second significant pillar is private insurance, which covers an additional 12.5% of the Jordanian population [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. This segment includes individuals who purchase policies independently and employees covered under institutional plans offered by private companies [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. Finally, a distinct category managed by UNRWA and NHUR provides services to refugees, covering 6.8% of the overall population, though these services are primarily focused on primary healthcare [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)].

It is crucial to note that various reports cite lower percentages for health insurance coverage, often focusing narrowly on the "civil health insurance" scheme administered by the Ministry of Health. One source states that 38% of Jordanians are covered by this specific program [[2](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/media/documents/2023-03/jordan_v3-compressed_1.pdf), [5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=czymn0kZbQM)]. Another report from late 2023 indicated that civil health insurance coverage reached 3,236,057 individuals, or 36.6% of the population, as of December 13th of the previous year [[7](https://www.instagram.com/p/DV1_osEDZvg/)]. These figures, while widely cited, should not be mistaken for the total national coverage rate. They represent only one part of the larger insurance landscape, specifically the mandatory scheme for public sector employees and their dependents. The comprehensive 76.80% figure is more appropriate for calculating an overall uninsured rate because it accounts for all major public, private, and specialized refugee insurance systems operating within the country [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. The discrepancy highlights a critical nuance in interpreting health statistics: the scope of the definition of "coverage." Focusing solely on civil health insurance provides a partial picture, whereas the holistic analysis offers a more complete understanding of the nation's healthcare safety net.

## The Non-Jordanian Population and Its Impact on Coverage Rates

The demographic composition of Jordan significantly influences any calculation of the overall uninsured rate. As of 2023, non-Jordanians constituted 26.08% of the country's total population of 11,517,887, numbering 3,003,818 individuals [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. This substantial minority group has a markedly different profile regarding health insurance coverage compared to the Jordanian citizen population. According to available data, only 38% of the non-Jordanian population holds any form of health insurance [[2](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/media/documents/2023-03/jordan_v3-compressed_1.pdf)]. This low rate of coverage is a critical factor when attempting to understand the broader landscape of healthcare access in the kingdom.

This 38% insurance rate among non-Jordanians stands in stark contrast to the 76.80% rate observed among Jordanians [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. If applied to the entire population, this disparity would pull the overall uninsured rate significantly higher than the 23.20% calculated for Jordanians alone. To illustrate, if we were to calculate a crude weighted average for the entire population based on these two groups, the result would be far greater than 23%. For instance, assuming the 38% coverage rate for non-Jordanians is accurate, their uninsured rate would be 62%. The combined effect on the national uninsured rate would be a function of both their low coverage and their large share of the total population. While the provided sources do not offer a single, unified national uninsured rate that explicitly combines these two distinct populations, the available data strongly suggests that the non-Jordanian community is a disproportionately large segment of the uninsured population nationwide.

The reasons for this lower insurance uptake are multifaceted and rooted in legal, economic, and social barriers faced by migrants and refugees. Access to the state-administered civil health insurance scheme, which covers a significant portion of Jordanians, is typically restricted to citizens and formal sector employees. Many non-citizens may work in the informal economy, making them ineligible for such programs. Furthermore, purchasing private insurance can be prohibitively expensive for individuals and families without stable, high-income employment. Legal residency status also plays a pivotal role; undocumented migrants face extreme barriers to accessing any formal healthcare system. While organizations like UNRWA provide essential primary care services to registered Palestinian refugees, many other non-Jordanian groups, including Syrian refugees and migrant workers, may fall through the cracks of the formal insurance net, relying instead on sporadic charity care or paying out-of-pocket for services, which can lead to catastrophic health expenditures. Therefore, any attempt to assess the situation in Amman, a major destination for migrants and refugees, must heavily account for the distinct and vulnerable position of this non-Jordanian population.

## Applying National Data to the Context of Amman

While the most reliable data points to a national uninsured rate of approximately 23.2% for Jordanians, applying this figure directly to the capital city of Amman introduces significant uncertainty. The validity of this national average as a precise proxy for Amman depends on whether the city's population characteristics mirror the national demographic and socioeconomic profile. The evidence suggests a complex interplay of factors that could either increase or decrease the uninsured rate in Amman relative to the national average.

On one hand, there are strong arguments suggesting that Amman's uninsured rate might be *lower* than the national average. As the political, administrative, and economic hub of Jordan, Amman hosts the headquarters of government ministries, large state-owned enterprises, and numerous international organizations. This concentration of formal sector employment means a higher proportion of the working population is likely covered by mandatory civil health insurance schemes or employer-sponsored private plans. The high population density often correlates with more developed infrastructure and service delivery networks, potentially facilitating easier enrollment and access to care for those enrolled in public programs. Given that the largest component of Jordan's insurance coverage comes from the government sector, and Amman is the epicenter of that sector, it is logical to hypothesize that insurance penetration is higher in the capital.

Conversely, several powerful countervailing forces suggest that the uninsured rate in Amman could be *higher* than the national average. Amman is the primary destination for internal and external migration seeking opportunities. This influx concentrates vulnerable populations, including refugees and migrants, who often have lower rates of formal insurance coverage due to precarious employment, lack of legal residency, or financial constraints. As established, the non-Jordanian population has a documented insurance coverage rate of just 38%, and their disproportionate concentration in the capital would elevate the city's overall uninsured rate beyond the 23.2% figure calculated for Jordanians alone [[2](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/media/documents/2023-03/jordan_v3-compressed_1.pdf)]. Furthermore, large urban centers like Amman tend to have larger informal economies. Workers in the informal sector are typically excluded from formal employer-sponsored or mandatory government insurance schemes, pushing them into the uninsured category. Without specific data disaggregating Amman's population by nationality, employment status, and income level, it is impossible to quantify the net effect of these opposing pressures. The absence of localized statistics from municipal health departments or specialized surveys on Amman's demographics makes any definitive conclusion speculative. Consequently, while the national figure of 23.2% uninsured Jordanians remains the best available quantitative benchmark, it must be understood as a foundational estimate with considerable geographic and demographic uncertainty when applied to the specific context of Amman.

## Synthesis and Final Estimate of Uninsured Rate

In direct response to the research goal of determining the most recent percentage of uninsured people in Amman, the analysis of available sources leads to a clear but nuanced conclusion. There is no specific, city-level statistic for the uninsured rate in Amman. Therefore, the most accurate approach is to use the most recent and comprehensive national-level data as the best available proxy, while transparently acknowledging its inherent limitations. Based on an analytical paper prepared for the World Bank, the most reliable estimate for the overall health insurance coverage rate for the Jordanian population in Jordan is 76.80% [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)]. This implies a national uninsured rate for Jordanians of 23.20%.

Given the lack of specific data for Amman, this 23.20% figure for uninsured Jordanians serves as the primary quantitative estimate. However, this number is incomplete as it does not account for the non-Jordanian population, who constitute over a quarter of the national population and exhibit a much lower insurance uptake rate of 38% [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/), [2](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/media/documents/2023-03/jordan_v3-compressed_1.pdf)]. The true national uninsured rate is therefore substantially higher than 23.20%. The final uninsured rate for Amman would depend entirely on the balance between these two groups within the city's population. If Amman's population mirrors the national average, the uninsured rate would reflect a weighted combination of these two distinct coverage profiles. If, however, Amman has a higher concentration of non-Jordanians or informal sector workers, the actual uninsured rate could be significantly higher than the 23.20% figure derived from Jordanian data alone.

In summary, the investigation yields the following key findings:
1.  **National Baseline:** The most robust national estimate indicates that 76.80% of Jordanians are covered by health insurance, leaving an uninsured rate of 23.20% for this group [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/)].
2.  **Non-Citizen Disparity:** The non-Jordanian population, making up a significant portion of the country, has a documented insurance coverage rate of only 38%, meaning 62% are uninsured [[1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11378627/), [2](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/sites/default/files/media/documents/2023-03/jordan_v3-compressed_1.pdf)].
3.  **Proxy Application:** Because specific data for Amman is unavailable, the national uninsured rate for Jordanians (approximately 23.2%) is the most reliable available figure to use as a proxy. It is critical to recognize this is an approximation.
4.  **Uncertainty Factors:** The applicability of this national figure to Amman is subject to significant uncertainty. The capital's unique status as a center for government employment could lead to lower uninsurance, while its role as a magnet for refugees and informal economy workers could lead to a higher rate.

Therefore, the final answer to the research question is that the most recent, best-estimated uninsured rate for residents of Amman is not definitively known from the provided sources. However, based on the available data, it is reasonable to conclude that a substantial portion of the population, approaching or exceeding 25%, lacks formal health insurance coverage. This estimate is anchored by the 23.2% uninsured rate for Jordanians nationally but is likely inflated by the high rates of uninsurance among the large non-Jordanian population concentrated in the capital.
